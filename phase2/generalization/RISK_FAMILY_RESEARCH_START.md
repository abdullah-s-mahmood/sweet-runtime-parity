# Phase 2 — Risk-Family Audit: Research Start

Date: 2026-09-29

CLEME2.0 (ACL 2025) argues that GEC evaluation should disentangle hit-correction, wrong-correction, under-correction and over-correction rather than collapse behavior into one score.

Arabic text-editing GEC (ACL 2025) shows that edit representation is central to efficient, interpretable Arabic correction and that ensembles can improve performance.

Edit-level majority voting (BEA 2026) further supports treating the edit—not the full generated sentence—as the unit of evidence.

These findings motivate a selective architecture:
exact agreement is first partitioned by edit family; each family may require a different acceptance contract.
